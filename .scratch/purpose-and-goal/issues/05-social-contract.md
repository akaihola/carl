# Social contract at the table

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 03

## Question

Who sees the fact card (only the owner, or everyone at the table), how are the others told the conversation is being processed, and what tone should a fact card take towards the speaker it contradicts?

## Answer

Settled with the owner in a grilling session.

- **Everyone sees the same card.** The phone is propped up for the whole table (see [Situations Carl serves](03-situations.md)), and every card is shown to everyone at the same moment. There is no owner-only or private mode: a hidden fact-checker working for one person at a table of friends is worse than an open one.
- **Disclosure.** The owner tells the table that Carl is listening when the conversation starts. If anyone objects, Carl doesn't run. While Carl listens, a **listening indicator** stays visible on screen the whole time, so late arrivals can see it too. Carl makes no spoken or ceremonial announcement of its own.
- **Anyone can pause.** Anyone at the table can **pause** Carl with one visible tap, e.g. when the talk turns to health, money or family. While paused, nothing is heard or kept, and the indicator shows the paused state. Only another tap resumes it, never a timer.
- **A card addresses the claim, never the person.** It gives the claim's gist in a few words, then the fact ("Einstein didn't fail maths: he excelled at it."). No speaker label, no name, no "you". Everyone heard who said it; pointing at them only adds sting.
- **Tone: neutral reference-book voice** for both claims and open questions. Plain, short, factual. No "Actually", no exclamation marks, no jokes, no emoji. In a hedged fact card only "probably"/"maybe" carries the uncertainty, with no extra softening. In Finnish, the neutral encyclopedic register.
