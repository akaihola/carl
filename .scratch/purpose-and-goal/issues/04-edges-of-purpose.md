# Edges of the purpose

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 02, 03

## Question

Are the two triggers (a false claim; an unanswered open question) the whole purpose, or do other kinds of fact card belong (a definition, a pertinent number, a misremembered quote)? And how wrong must a claim be before it earns a fact card: where is the pedantry threshold?

## Answer

Settled with the owner in a grilling session.

- **Only the two triggers.** A fact card is shown for a false claim or an open question, nothing else.
  - A misremembered quote ("as Churchill said…") is a false claim.
  - Confirming a true claim counts only when the table actually disagrees ("was it 1953 or '54?"). That makes it an open question.
  - Definitions, pertinent numbers and "did you know" asides are out. They would make Carl a chatty companion.
- **Pedantry threshold.** A claim gets a card only when the error changes the point being made, or when it repeats a well-known myth ("Einstein failed maths"). Loose approximations ("Helsinki has about a million people"), off-by-one details that don't matter to the point, and claims that are true under a reasonable reading get nothing.
- **Confidence bands (reconciles "hedge, don't refuse" with "precision over recall").**
  - Confident verdict: the card states it plainly.
  - Likely verdict: a *hedged fact card* ("probably", "maybe").
  - Anything below: nothing is shown.
  - A card always needs a citable source. No source means nothing is shown, however likely the verdict seems.
  - The same bands apply to both triggers.
  - `AGENTS.md`'s "never surface uncertain verdicts" becomes "never surface unsourced or low-confidence verdicts; hedge moderate ones".
  - How confidence is measured belongs to the vendor effort (out of scope).
- **Checkable means checkable against a public source.** Opinions, predictions, private or personal facts ("I was born in Tampere") and contested questions with no settled answer are out. On contested topics Carl stays silent rather than taking a side.
- **The table comes first.** If someone at the table corrects the claim or answers the open question before the card is ready, Carl drops it. A "correction" that is itself false is simply a new claim.
- **Overhear only.** No wake word and no direct questions to Carl. A question asked out loud and left unanswered is already an open question.

Glossary updated in `CONTEXT.md`: *Claim* (checkable against a public source), *Open question* (left unresolved by the table), new term *Hedged fact card*.
